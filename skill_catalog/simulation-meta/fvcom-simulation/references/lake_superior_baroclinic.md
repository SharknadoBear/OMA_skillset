# Lake Superior baroclinic temperature, wind and river workflow

The [lake validation CLI](lake_validation.md) joins full-depth native station outputs to observed temperature, IGLD85 water level and GLSEA lake-mean surface temperature. It also produces a separate NOAA model-reference comparison.

Run the [spinup and budget reducers](lake_physics.md) on native stage outputs to select the July restart and reconcile heat and water storage with atmospheric and river forcing.

Choose `mode=baroclinic-temperature-wind-river`, `profile=lake_superior_summer` and `execution.site=perlmutter`. Provide the selected CPU charging account and the study-owned absolute scratch and source directories. Run Control's `init` writes the July 2025 stage schedule and role workspaces. Select this workflow before the general tide preparation and validation steps.

1. The grid worker freezes the retained spherical LSOFS mesh, per-node positive-down depths, 21 vertical interfaces, islands, closed boundary and the native river edges. The forcing worker invokes `great-lakes-ofs-fetcher` and `fvcom-initialization` for exact UTC 3-D state, and owns the wind, pressure, direct radiation and heat, P/E and river forcing handoff. The build worker configures a pressure-enabled freshwater 3-D executable, uses `perlmutter-hpc` to compile and verifies the density maximum and signed outlet tracer flux. Start these roles concurrently. Link the grid, runtime pressure and compiler definitions to the startup manifest before launch.
2. Verify common mesh and layer identities, UTC range, binary, active reader variables, dates, forcing hashes, station locations and depth datum. Use `fvcom_namelist.py lake` for the final namelist. For surface forcing choose HRRR, ERA5, CFSv2, then CFSR in archive era. Keep signed St. Marys discharge and local donor heat/salt, precipitation and evaporation in the water budget. Run the `lake-attempt` launcher with one thread per rank and its frozen input hashes. Retain daily restarts, hourly station data and six-hourly 3-D fields.
3. Smoke-test July 1–2 using 128 ranks. Inspect time zero and all forcing readers, then continue from the July 2 native restart. Start separate 7-, 14- and 28-day trials from the dated June 24, 17 and 3 NOAA analyses. Compare July 1–4 on common native supports, including volume-weighted temperature, upper-20-m temperature, thermocline depth and mean elevation. Use the predeclared daily limits in the stage plan. Choose the shortest duration agreeing with every longer physically valid trial. If sensitivity persists use the verified 28-day July 1 restart and state the uncertainty.
4. Benchmark the identical July 1–2 segment on 64, 128 and 256 ranks, three replicates per layout. Choose the fastest complete layout within 15% of minimum median charged node-hours. Use the selected spin-up restart and layout for July 1–August 1. Verify native completion, clock, checkpoint and finite fields at each stage. Compare volume and heat storage with applied fluxes, sample observed temperatures at sensor depth, use the documented lake datum for gauge comparisons and compare lake-wide surface temperatures with GLSEA. Keep NOAA model-to-model diagnostics distinct from independent observations.

After validation, publish the tested Lake Superior configuration and a compact case record with the spin-up choice, benchmark result, observation coverage and remaining model errors in the OMA `fvcom-simulation` skill. Commit and push the tested changes, synchronize the local Codex installation, and verify its files against the published revision. Store native NetCDF inputs, model fields and restarts with the study.

For this profile, the forcing worker invokes `ranked_lake_surface.py --lake-profile lake_superior_summer`. Supply the parent integration dates as `--rain-context` for each subset. ### July 2025 heat-budget qualification

Reproduce the limiter-off sensitivity test with `--no-scalar-positivity-control` and `NOFLUX_BOT_CONDITION=T`. Two 128-rank July 1–2 runs held the executable, initialization and forcing fixed. Disabling the scalar limiter moved the wet-domain heat residual from −30.58 to −3.68 W/m². Both runs preserved the wet masks and closed the one-day water budget within 0.0001 mm of lake-equivalent depth. In the limiter-off run, global samples every 500 model seconds ranged from 2.117 to 26.332 °C. The 24-hour volume-weighted difference between settings was 0.052 °C RMS for 3-D temperature, with a local maximum difference of 3.96 °C.

The June 24 and June 17 trials failed the multi-day freshwater temperature check. At 48 hours, both native restarts showed subfreezing water at the same wet Sturgeon Bay shoreline node (48.55243° N, 88.43671° W, chart depth 0.10 m). Its upper layer reached −3.105 and −2.879 °C, respectively. Across the lake, about 387,000 and 46,000 m³ were more than 0.01 °C below their local freshwater freezing points. A six-hourly June 25 field had already reached −8.126 °C there. The two attempts were stopped with their checkpoints retained. The 28-day trial was withdrawn before allocation.

Treat the limiter-off setting as a diagnostic sensitivity. The July 1 smoke qualifies the startup, readers, restart and one-day execution. For spin-up qualification, evaluate wet-layer temperatures against the local pressure-dependent freezing point on every stored clock, including closed restarts. Inspect affected volume, duration and column energy. In shallow water, quantify shortwave reaching the bed, the atmospheric interpolation weights coming from land, and the signed water heat budget. Require a physically passing multi-day case before selecting a July restart, benchmarking or publishing month-long skill scores.

### Lake-supported HRRR qualification

The original Sturgeon Bay heat stencil drew 85.8% of its weight from land. All 24 nodes with a freezing excursion in the two June trials had some land influence. The repeat runs sampled direct heat and vapour flux from the connected Lake Superior water body. The executable, initial conditions, surface stress, pressure, river inputs, bathymetry, optical coefficients and scalar limiter setting matched their original controls. Native wind stress and pressure were bitwise identical on every target and clock. Rainfall matched to storage precision after accounting for condensation.

Every saved wet layer passed its local freezing check. At hour 42 of the June 24 run, the lake-wide wet minimum rose from −8.126 to 2.656 °C. At the end of the June 17 run, the upper layer at the original cold node reached 24.84 °C, compared with 16.29 °C in the NOAA nowcast. On July 1 the revised lake-wide surface mean was 15.34 °C against 7.54 °C from daily GLSEA. Retain the shoreline warm error and the broader initialization bias when interpreting the longer experiments.

The three independent heat and water reductions completed on Perlmutter (62010787, 62010786 and 62011207 in table order). At the original Sturgeon Bay node, the June 17 and June 24 post-initialization temperature RMSEs against NOAA changed from 10.13 to 6.54 °C and from 11.55 to 1.13 °C, respectively. The second corrected June run ended at 12.94 °C against 15.40 °C in NOAA. The two June heat residuals were −2.98 and −3.12 W/m². During six hours on June 24 the revised and original water budgets had similar positive residuals of 0.837 and 0.842 million m³. The revised residual was 0.0102 mm in lake-equivalent depth. The model briefly dried several elements between two fully wet output records. The cumulative volume from its minimum-depth correction was unavailable. Report such a residual explicitly even when daily restarts and gridded elevations agree.

If the water-supported case still develops a shallow temperature error, inspect the HRRR donor's skin temperature alongside the modeled surface, and evaluate signed sensible, latent and longwave heat at both SSTs using the same atmosphere. In a standalone freshwater COARE26Z calculation at June 19 00 UTC (job 62013339), the water donor's skin was 9.81 °C. Prescribed net heat was +71 W/m² into the lake. The bulk formula returned −435 W/m² at 24.84 °C and −102 W/m² at 16.29 °C. At the June 26 endpoint, prescribed and bulk fluxes at the modeled SST were −54 and −55 W/m². This sensitivity measures instantaneous feedback. A change to coupled heat exchange also requires consistent vapor mass, a wind-speed input and its own matched runs and restart tests.

The longer June 24 experiment (Perlmutter 62012996) failed the native thermal check at a different location on its third day. At June 26 12 UTC, the upper layer at wet Chequamegon Bay node 694 was −1.994 °C in 0.213 m of water. The collocated NOAA nowcast was +13.976 °C. Across the lake, 356 wet node/layer pairs totaling 162,368 m³ were more than 0.1 °C below their local freezing point. At 18 UTC the substantial cold volume reached 246,695 m³, and 367 node/layer pairs had crossed the 0.01 °C margin on both consecutive records. At noon the water-supported heat flux at node 694 was −129.72 W/m² and the original mixed interpolation would have given −126.92 W/m². The shoreline donor correction fixed the original Sturgeon Bay cases. This prescribed-heat configuration failed the longer Chequamegon Bay test and its 7-day trial is ineligible for restart selection.
